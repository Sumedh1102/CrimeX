## Intended use

- Decision support for police-station analysts: which zones, crime types and time bands show elevated predicted risk for the coming week, and why.
- Output is always `Risk(zone, crime_type, time_window)` for aggregated 1.5 km grid zones. The model never scores individuals, households or demographic groups.
- Predictions represent statistical patterns in historical reported incident data and are intended for analytical decision support. They are not guarantees of future criminal activity.

## Out of scope

- Any person-level prediction, profiling or targeting.
- Automated enforcement decisions; an officer remains the decision-maker.
- Claims that an incident *will* occur, or causal claims about places.
- Use on real data without re-validation (the current model is trained on synthetic data).

## Targets

- **Classification (deployed):** At least one reported incident of the crime type in the zone during the time band on any day of the forecast window.
- **Regression:** Number of reported incidents of the crime type in the zone during the time band over the forecast window.

## Training procedure

- Chronological splits by forecast-window start with a boundary embargo (windows straddling a split date are dropped). Train 2022-02-01 → 2024-12-31 (152 weekly windows); validation 2025-01-07 → 2025-12-30 (51); test 2026-01-06 → 2026-09-01 (34).
- Origins need 56 weeks of history, so the first year only feeds features.
- Percentile references for the F and S components are fitted on training origins only.
- XGBoost (`binary:logistic`) and an XGBoost Poisson regressor are trained on the training split; the classifier is calibrated with isotonic regression on validation.
- A Random Forest (calibrated the same way), a historical-rate baseline, the explainable CRS and a logistic fit on the CRS components are evaluated on identical windows.
- The recency half-life of component R is tuned per crime type on validation (CRS PR-AUC).
- XGBoost parameters: `{'n_estimators': 400, 'learning_rate': 0.05, 'max_depth': 6, 'min_child_weight': 5.0, 'subsample': 0.8, 'colsample_bytree': 0.8, 'reg_lambda': 1.0, 'tree_method': 'hist', 'n_jobs': None}`

## Findings on this run

- The calibrated XGBoost beats the historical-rate baseline on every probability metric but only modestly (test PR-AUC 0.313 vs 0.303; top-10% capture 39.4% vs 37.3%). Most of the synthetic signal is stable spatial concentration, which a long-run rate already captures; the gains come from emerging hotspots, surges and rare crime types.
- The Random Forest is statistically indistinguishable from XGBoost (PR-AUC 0.313). Deep spatiotemporal models are not justified until they beat this baseline on real data.
- The hand-weighted CRS ranks less well when pooled across crime types (PR-AUC 0.162) because its F and S components are percentiles within each crime type; within a crime type it separates well (see the per-type table). It is kept as the explanation layer, not as the most accurate ranker.
- Calibration on the held-out period is close to the diagonal (see the table above).

## Ethical considerations and limitations

- **Synthetic training data.** Patterns were planted by the generator; real reported data will contain reporting bias, under-reporting and recording practices that the synthetic data does not.
- **Feedback loops.** Directing attention to flagged zones can raise recorded incidents there. Monitor with officer feedback and periodic re-evaluation before any operational use.
- **Association, not causation.** Affinity and risk describe historical reported patterns.
- **Small counts.** Rare heads (e.g. Robbery Chain Snatching) have very low base rates; their probabilities carry wide uncertainty, reflected in the LOW confidence label.
- **Scope.** Zone-level only; no personal, demographic or individual data is used.
